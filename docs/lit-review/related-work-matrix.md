## Synthesis (what the matrix shows)

1. **Initial generation is covered.** WebGen-Bench, FullStackBench, AppWorld, DevBench
   (#18–#21, #24) evaluate what AI systems can *build*. None of them runs a repair loop
   against deterministic multi-category QA feedback.
2. **Repair against tests is covered for repos and issues.** SWE-bench and its
   successors (#1–#3, #5–#7) verify patches with unit tests, including PASS_TO_PASS
   regressions, on Python repositories. Our setting differs in artifact (whole
   full-stack apps from commercial builders), oracles (browser-level functional,
   security, accessibility, build, persistence), and driver (deterministic feedback
   generated by tools, delivered to the same commercial builder that produced the app).
3. **Regression-after-repair is a named, measured problem in classical APR** (#8–#11).
   Our study measures its modern analogue in LLM-driven app repair, where the literature
   is thin.
4. **Iterative degradation exists as a concern** (#4, #15, #16) but is studied at
   function or reasoning level, or with non-deterministic self-feedback.
5. **Security measurement of AI-generated code exists** (#12–#14) but not as part of a
   repair-loop trajectory on full applications.
6. **Vibe-coding app builders are compared in vendor blogs, not in peer-reviewed
   empirical studies** (search s15 returned only commercial comparisons). This is the
   single clearest coverage hole the search exposed.
